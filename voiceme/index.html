VoiceMe

An iPad app for kids who can't speak but can read and type. Write a word or a sentence, press Speak, and the iPad says it. The words you use most stay a tap away.

See the roadmap Follow along

Status: early build, not on the App Store yet.

Interactive sketch. Speech uses your browser's voice; the app uses Apple's on-device voices.

What it does

Type, then speak

A full keyboard and one big Speak button. Fast, clear speech that works with no internet.

Word and phrase bank

Star favorites, group phrases, and keep the most-used ones on top. History size is adjustable.

History you can recall

Say it again with one tap instead of typing it twice.

Audit log for caregivers

A record of what was said, so parents, teachers and therapists can see how it's being used.

Accessible from the start

Built around iPad accessibility, with Switch Control and Guided Access support planned.

Keyboard help

Accommodations for kids who read well but type slowly, including predictive text.

The build plan

Nine phases, each one shippable on its own. The first goal was simple: type something and hear it. Everything else layers on top.

Accessibility isn't a phase at the end. It's a rule for every phase.

Built with

    Why I'm building it

    Communication apps for non-verbal kids are often expensive, cluttered, or built around picture grids that a fluent reader outgrows. VoiceMe is for the child who knows exactly what they want to say and just needs it said, quickly and in their own words.

    Your words stay on your iPad. VoiceMe has no accounts, no ads and no tracking. Read the privacy policy.

    Want to help?

    If you're a parent, speech-language pathologist or teacher who works with AAC users, I'd love to hear what matters to you. Early testers welcome once there's a TestFlight build.
